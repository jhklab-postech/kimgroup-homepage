Van der Waals quantum materials offer unprecedented control over layered structures with atomically thin and sharply defined interfaces. By engineering vdW stacking sequences and twist angles, it is possible to confine electrons within tunable atomic-scale and periodic potentials that do not exist in naturally occurring crystals. This level of structural control fundamentally reshapes light–matter interactions, enabling the enhancement and manipulation of optical processes such as light absorption, scattering, and emission. Our research seeks to uncover how these engineered electronic states interact with light at ultrafast time scales and to establish design principles for controlling optical responses in vdW quantum materials.
